Backtest

Check whether companies with low or high values of a ratio did better in the following year.

Returns

Each year on 31 March the companies are ranked by one ratio, split into five equal groups and held for a year. The 200 companies were chosen as the largest at the end of 2024, so every group shows high returns: compare the groups with each other and with the benchmark, not with the market (methodology (opens in a new tab)). Historical data for learning, not investment advice.

How the backtest works
Ranking
The values the screener shows on 31 March of each year from 2017 to 2025: that day's close and each company's latest annual report filed before that session.
Quintiles
Five groups of equal size; quintile 1 holds the lowest P/E, P/B and P/S, or the highest value of every other ratio. Equal values keep the ticker order, as in the screener.
Left out
A company without a value, and a value marked Spin-off, Share issue or Discontinued ops for that ratio. FCF margin, OCF / net income, ROA and equity / assets leave out financial firms (SIC codes 6000–6999), whose cash flow, assets and leverage mean something else. Operating margin is not ranked: it is empty for whole sectors.
Purchase and sale
Bought in equal parts at the adjusted close of the next session, which includes dividends, and held without rebalancing until the next year's purchase. The last holding ends on 31 Dec 2025 and is shown on its own.
Benchmark
Equal parts of every ranked company, so the difference between quintiles measures the ranking, not the set of companies. All 200 is the whole catalogue in equal parts.
Costs
0.10% of every trade at each yearly purchase: companies sold, bought and brought back to an equal part. The final sale is free.
Per year
The compounded return of the full years, as a yearly rate.
Mean 1 − 5 and t
The test of the ranking, as studies of portfolio sorts report it: the monthly returns of quintile 1 minus quintile 5, before costs, averaged and multiplied by 12 to show them per year, and t, that average divided by its standard error. Only a t well away from zero, about 2 or more either way, tells the ranking from chance, and with eleven ratios one of them can reach 2 by chance. The mean differs from the yearly differences in the table and from quintile 1's rate per year minus quintile 5's: those compound the months, which lowers a quintile's rate the more its monthly returns swing.
Random p
The share of 1,000 random portfolios of the quintile's size, from the same ranked companies and before costs, that did at least as well; the years are combined with Stouffer's method. It tells how the quintile did against random picks in these years, including any bet on a style that moved its companies together, so it can be small while t shows nothing.
Years 1 > 5
The number of full years in which quintile 1 did better than quintile 5, before costs.
Very large values
A ratio with a small positive denominator can be very large: OCF / net income with thin profits, EPS growth after a weak year, ROE with little equity after share buybacks. Quintile 1 of these ratios therefore also holds such companies. Little equity makes P/B large too, so quintile 5 of P/B holds them.
