Backtest

Check whether companies with low or high values of a ratio did better in the following year.

Returns

Each year on 31 March the companies are ranked by one ratio, split into five equal groups and held for a year. The 200 companies were chosen as the largest at the end of 2024, so every group shows high returns: compare the groups with each other and with the benchmark, not with the market (methodology (opens in a new tab)). Historical data for learning, not investment advice.

How the backtest works
Ranking
The screener's values on 31 March of 2017–2025: that day's close and the latest annual report filed before it.
Quintiles
Five equal groups; quintile 1 holds the lowest P/E, P/B and P/S and the highest value of every other ratio.
Left out
Companies without a value or with a Spin-off, Share issue or Discontinued ops flag on it. FCF margin, OCF / net income, ROA and equity / assets also leave out financial firms, whose cash flow, assets and leverage mean something else. Operating margin is not ranked: it is empty for whole sectors.
Purchase and sale
Bought in equal parts at the next session's adjusted close, dividends included, and held without rebalancing for a year.
Benchmark
Every ranked company in equal parts, so a quintile is compared with the set it came from. All 200 is the whole catalogue.
Costs
0.10% of every trade at each yearly purchase, including bringing holdings back to equal parts.
Per year
The compounded return of the full years, as a yearly rate.
Volatility and worst drop
How much the monthly returns swing, per year (standard deviation × √12), and the largest fall from the highest value so far, from daily closes. Both before costs, over the full years.
Mean 1 − 5 and t-stat
The test of the ranking, as in studies of portfolio sorts: the average monthly return of quintile 1 minus quintile 5, before costs, times 12. The t-stat is that average over its standard error: only about ±2 or beyond tells the ranking from chance, and with eleven ratios one can get there by chance. The mean differs from the yearly differences and from Q1's rate minus Q5's, which compound the months and so lower a quintile that swings more.
Random p
The share of 1,000 random portfolios of the quintile's size, from the same ranked companies, that did at least as well, before costs and with the years combined. Near 0 the quintile beat random picks, near 1 it lagged them. A style that moved its companies together can make it small while the t-stat shows nothing.
Years 1 > 5
Full years in which quintile 1 beat quintile 5, before costs.
Very large values
A small positive denominator makes a ratio very large: OCF / net income with thin profits, EPS growth after a weak year, ROE and P/B with little equity after buybacks. Such companies land in quintile 1, and in quintile 5 for P/B.
